## Ingredients

- 40 g skinless hazelnuts , roasted (Note 1)
- 20 g blanched almonds (ie skinless almonds), substitute skin-on, or walnuts, cashews or more pistachios (Note 2)
- 20 g pistachios kernels , unsalted – substitute more almonds, or walnuts or cashews
- 2 tbsp white sesame seeds
- 1 1/2 tsp fennel seeds (substitute 1/2 tsp ground, I'd still toast to bring out flavour)

#### Spices:
- 1/2 tsp cumin powder
- 1/2 tsp coriander powder
- 1/4 tsp cayenne pepper – feel free to reduce or omit
- 1/4 heaped teaspoon cooking salt

## Directions

1. Preheat oven to 180°C/350°F (160°C fan-forced).
2. Roast nuts – Spread the hazelnuts, almonds and pistachios on a tray. Bake for 5 minutes.
3. Roast seeds – Add sesame seeds and fennel seeds to the tray. Shake the tray to spread evenly. Bake for 3 minutes.
4. Coarsely blitz – Cool on the tray for 15 minutes. Pour everything into a mini food processor. (Note 4) Add the Spices. Blitz until coarsely ground – don't let it become a powder. (Or do this in a mortar and pestle, or in a ziplock bag and bash using a rolling pin or meat mallet). Note – the chunkier nut bits will rise to the surface and powder will sink, so don't be tricked into thinking it's chunkier than it is!
5. Use as a bread dipper with olive oil or sprinkling over "everything"! Use generously, not a sparse sprinkle, defeats the purpose!

#### Note:
- As a bread dip (traditional) – Pour dukkah into a small shallow bowl and a good extra virgin olive oil in another (my favourite shop-bought is Lomondo from Mudgee in NSW). Pile warm cubes of bread, focaccia or wedges of pita bread on the side. Dip bread into olive oil first then the dukkah (it will stick to the oil). Eat!
- Dukkah bread – Brush bread with olive oil then sprinkle with dukkah and sea salt flakes. Ideal to use bread with a large surface area (eg pita bread, Lebanese bread, focaccia)
- Crumbing – Add to a breadcrumb mixture, like these Crumbed Baked Chicken Tenders or Schnitzel!
- *Note on cooking* If using dukkah to sprinkle on things that go into the oven, be mindful of the nuts burning. Best to use for things that have a short bake time (eg on bread, ~10 min in oven) or mix it with breadcrumbs. Otherwise, use to sprinkle on the cooked food.
- Topping or finish – Sprinkle generously over foods to make a good dish gourmet, or to make simple foods instantly more exciting! Examples: Garlic prawns, roasted vegetables, glazed carrots, oven baked chicken breast, a store bought roast chicken (add olive oil as well), pan fried fish, avocado toast, scrambled eggs. It'd be spectacular sprinkled on these Sweet Potato Steaks or Maple Roasted Carrots with Whipped Tahini (instead of pine nuts). (Remember – use generously!)
- Garnish for dips – like hummus, whipped feta and labne.
